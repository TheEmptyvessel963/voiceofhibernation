We like to think that when confronted with new evidence, we update our beliefs rationally. The research says otherwise.

## The Updating Problem

Psychologist Philip Tetlock spent decades studying expert prediction. His finding: even domain experts are barely better than chance at forecasting events in their field. The reason? Most of us reason **toward** conclusions we already hold, rather than **from** evidence.

This is called **motivated reasoning**, and it's the default mode of the human mind.

## What Good Updating Looks Like

Good belief updating requires three things:

1. **Clear prior beliefs** — you need to know what you believed before.
2. **Genuine engagement** with disconfirming evidence, not dismissal.
3. **Calibration** — matching confidence to the strength of evidence.

> "The test of a first-rate intelligence is the ability to hold two opposed ideas in mind at the same time and still retain the ability to function." — F. Scott Fitzgerald

## A Practice

Each week, pick one belief you hold confidently. Find the strongest argument *against* it. See what happens.

The goal isn't to flip-flop. It's to make sure your beliefs are earned.
